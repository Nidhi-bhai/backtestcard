Exam Countdown & Study Planner

Enter your exam date and what you need to cover. You get the days and study hours left, then a day-by-day plan that splits time by how heavy each subject is and keeps the final days free for revision.

How the plan is built

Days left = exam date minus today. Rest days are taken out each week, then the last few days are kept for full revision and past papers. The remaining study days are shared between subjects in proportion to their weight, and subjects are interleaved (rotated) instead of done in one block, because switching topics improves retention for most learners.

Making the countdown realistic

  • Plan 80% of your real hours. Things go wrong, and a plan you can't keep tends to get abandoned.
  • Put the heaviest subject in your best-focus hours.
  • Use the revision days for timed past papers, not new material.

FAQ

How many days are left until my exam?

Pick your exam date above; the planner counts whole days from today and shows the study hours you realistically have.

How do I make a study plan for exams?

List subjects, rate how much work each needs, set your daily hours, reserve the final days for revision, then split the remaining days by weight. This tool does that automatically.

How many hours a day should I study for exams?

It depends on the exam and your stamina. 4–6 focused hours is sustainable for most students; above that, quality drops for many people.

Should I study one subject per day?

Rotating subjects across days (interleaving) usually helps retention more than finishing one subject in a long block.
