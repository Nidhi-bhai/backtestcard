Why this exists

If you earn, spend or plan in dollars, or you just want to compare honestly, an Indian portfolio has two risks: the market and the rupee. US backtesters ignore the rupee. Indian tools usually ignore the dollar. BacktestCard puts both on one screen, using data you can check yourself.

What you get

  • Crisis replays of the dot-com bust, 2008, the 2013 taper tantrum, the 2020 COVID crash and the 2022 rate shock: how far your mix fell and how long it took to recover.
  • USD or INR results, with currency conversion applied consistently to every asset.
  • Share cards: a PNG plus a link that reopens the exact same test.
  • Embeds for blogs and newsletters: one iframe, free.
  • Bring your own data: upload a NIFTY TRI, a mutual-fund NAV or anything else as CSV, and it is converted for you.

Method and honest limits

Where does the data come from?
US Federal Reserve (H.10 rupee rate, H.15 Treasury yields), US BLS (CPI), the OECD (India and US share price indices, India interest rates) and the World Bank Pink Sheet (gold). All are fetched from the original publishers under terms that allow reuse with attribution. See DATA_SOURCES.md.
Why not NIFTY 50 or the S&P 500 directly?
Their index data is licensed and can't be redistributed for free. The OECD's national share price indices track the broad market instead. If you have the right to use an index file, upload it, and it stays on your device.
What's missing?
Dividends: equity series are price-only, which understates stock returns by roughly 1–2% a year. Taxes. Intra-month moves: monthly averages make crashes look shallower than the daily lows. Bond returns are modelled from 10-year yields, not taken from a real fund. India bond and cash data start in Dec 2011.
Is this advice?
No. It's an educational calculator of past data. It doesn't rank portfolios or tell you what to buy.
Is my data private?
Yes. There's no account, no tracking script and no server; everything runs in your browser.
